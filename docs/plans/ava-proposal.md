## What growth means here

- ABRAM is production operations software. Scheduling, crewing, budgets, deliverables, invoicing,
  all in one place, for people who currently run productions out of spreadsheets and group chats.
- Growth means finding the people who have that problem, showing them the thing, and getting them to
  pay for it. It does not mean brand awareness, it does not mean impressions, and it does not mean
  content for its own sake.
- Five segments, in the order I think they matter. You are free to argue the order after 30 days of
  actually talking to them.
  - **Production companies and studios.** The core. Multi-project, multi-crew, real budgets. Highest
    value, longest cycle.
  - **Freelancers.** Solo operators running their own jobs. Fast to close, low value each, and they
    become the crew on somebody else's production later, which is how the network compounds.
  - **Agencies.** In-house content teams at brands and agencies. Similar shape to studios, different
    language, they care about client approvals and deliverable tracking more than crew.
  - **Universities and film schools.** $400 per student per year on a four year program licence.
    Long procurement, annual budget cycles, and the students carry us into the industry when they
    graduate. This is the segment I would most like you to crack.
  - **Creators and influencers.** They come in through the Link Hub and the self-serve funnel. Low
    touch by design. Your job here is the funnel, not the individual conversation.
- Two motions, and they do not share a playbook.
  - **Self serve.** Someone finds us, signs up, pays. Your levers are content, tracked links, promo
    codes and the funnel.
  - **Enterprise.** Someone gets a demo and a conversation. Your levers are outbound, conferences
    and follow up.

## What you own, end to end

- **Lead generation.** Outbound, conferences, content, the mailing list, tracked links, promo codes,
  social. If it brings a name into the CRM, it is yours.
- **Qualification.** Deciding what is real. I would rather you disqualify fast and loudly than carry
  a dead account for a quarter.
- **Demo.** You run it. I will do the first ten with you and then only join when you want me there
  or the deal needs me.
- **Close.** Yours to run. Anything with a non-standard price, a custom contract, or a legal redline
  comes to me before you say yes.
- **Handoff.** Once they pay, you introduce them to onboarding and you stay the relationship owner
  through their first 90 days. Churn inside 90 days is partly a sales problem, not only a product
  problem.
- **The number.** Attributed MRR sourced by you. That is the one metric your job is measured on.

## What stays with me

- Product. What gets built, in what order, and what it looks like.
- Pricing. The plans, the tiers, the discounts that are allowed to exist.
- Contracts and anything legal.
- Stripe, billing, refunds, and every payment record. You will never need access to it and you will
  never be asked to touch it.
- Platform administration. Other people's organisations, their data, their entitlements. That is a
  separate system and it is not something either of us should be casual about.
- Sending the broadcast. You write it. I press send. Not a trust issue, a blast radius issue: the
  sending domain reputation takes months to rebuild and the recipients cannot un-receive it.

## How we work

- **The CRM is the system of record.** If it is not in the CRM it did not happen. Not because I want
  a paper trail on you, but because commission is calculated from it and I want the number to be a
  lookup rather than an argument.
- **Every touch gets logged.** Call, meeting, email, demo, note. The one to one email tool logs
  itself. Everything else is one line in the timeline.
- **Deal registration.** Before you work an account nobody here has spoken to, register it. You file
  it, I have five business days to say no. If I say nothing, it is yours. It stays yours for 120 days.
  That is how we make sure neither of us ever discovers we were both working the same company.
- **Attribution is not a conversation.** Three rules, in order: your promo code redeemed at checkout,
  your tracked link recorded at signup, or a named account you registered in writing before anyone
  here contacted them and closed inside 120 days. First match wins. There is no override, including
  from me. The system shows you every rule it tested and why each one did or did not fire.
- **Weekly pipeline review.** 30 minutes, same slot every week. We go through the board. What moved,
  what is stuck, what you need from me.
- **Monthly commission statement.** Generated from the ledger, in the console, visible to you at any
  time. Paid within 30 days of the close of each collection month.
- **Ask early.** If you are three days into something and unsure, that is two days too long.

## Compensation

Proposed. Say what does not work and we will change it before either of us signs anything.

- **Base or draw: [base].** My proposal is a modest monthly [draw] recoverable against commission,
  rather than a salary, for the first [X] months. It means you are not living on nothing while the
  pipeline builds, and it does not put a fixed cost on a pre-revenue company. If you would rather
  have a real base and a lower rate, say so, that trade is available.
- **Commission on first year value of deals you sourced.**
  - **[20]% when you sourced it and closed it.**
  - **[10]% when you sourced it and I closed it.**
  - Both rates require that you sourced it. Closing an account I originated is not a commission
    event. I want to be direct about that rather than have it surface in month four.
- **Paid on cash actually collected, not on bookings.** A deal that signs for $30,000 and has
  collected $2,400 pays you on $2,400. As the rest arrives, you get paid on the rest.
- **Basis is gross less any discount applied at checkout.** Payment processing fees are not deducted
  from your basis.
- **Tail: [12] months.** A customer keeps paying you commission for 12 months from their first
  payment, then stops.
- **Clawback: [90] days.** A refund, chargeback or cancellation inside 90 days reverses the
  commission on it. It appears as a negative line on the next statement, never as a quiet edit to a
  statement you have already seen.
- **EDU multi-year contracts pay on cash received, per invoice.** A four year program licence billed
  annually pays you each year the money lands, inside the 12 month tail from that customer's first
  payment. I am open to a different structure here if the tail makes multi-year work unattractive,
  because those are the deals I most want you chasing.
- **Excluded from commission:** comped accounts, accounts I was already managing before you started,
  and anything under [carve-out], all named up front and visible on the account record rather than
  decided afterwards.
- **Equity: [X]% total, in tranches.** [Y]% on signing, the rest triggered at attributed MRR
  thresholds of [$A] and [$B]. Standard [4] year vest, [1] year cliff. The instrument is paper. The
  console tracks whether a threshold has been crossed and when, so we are reading the same number.
- **Ramp targets.**
  - **First 30 days:** no revenue target. 50 qualified conversations, the CRM populated and honest,
    one written point of view on which segment you think we should attack first and why.
  - **First 60 days:** [3] closed deals of any size, 10 live opportunities, one EDU conversation
    reaching procurement.
  - **First 90 days:** [$X] attributed MRR, a pipeline worth [3x] the next quarter's target, and a
    repeatable outbound motion written down well enough that a third person could run it.
- **Review at 90 days**, both directions. If it is working we move you from advisor terms to employed
  terms. If it is not, we say so plainly and part on good terms.

## Tools and access

You get a login to the ABRAM marketing console with the Growth role.

- **CRM.** The full pipeline. Contacts, companies, deals, activity timeline, tasks. You edit the
  accounts you own or sourced.
- **Deal registration.** File a claim on a named account.
- **One to one email.** Send from a contact's record. It logs itself to their timeline and tracks
  opens and clicks.
- **Campaign drafts.** Write broadcasts to the mailing list. I send them.
- **Subscribers.** Read only. Enough to size a segment, not enough to change anybody's consent.
- **Tracked links and promo codes.** Yours to create. They are also how attribution proves itself, so
  use them.
- **Link Hub and campaign landing pages.**
- **Social Studio.** Post images, carousels, the calendar.
- **Blog and help docs.** Full write access. Content is an acquisition channel and I would rather you
  publish than ask.
- **Website analytics.**
- **Your earnings page.** Your statement, your rates, every entry with the rate that applied, at any
  time without asking me.

What you do not get, and why:

- **The platform admin console.** Other customers' organisations and data. Nobody should have casual
  access to that, including me on a normal day.
- **Stripe and billing.** Not yours, not needed, and keeping you clear of it keeps the commission
  ledger clean.
- **Broadcast send.** Draft yes, send no.
- **Role management.** Nobody creates logins but me.
- **Release notes.** A changelog entry is a product claim.

## Cadence and reporting

- **Daily:** work the CRM. Nothing formal.
- **Weekly, [Monday]:** 30 minute pipeline review. You drive, I ask questions. Anything you need from
  me comes out of this meeting.
- **Weekly, written:** three lines in Slack. What moved, what is stuck, what you need. Not a report,
  a signal.
- **Monthly:** commission statement lands. Numbers review: sourced MRR, closed deals, conversion by
  stage, which channel produced what.
- **Quarterly:** what we stop doing. This one matters more than the others. A two person company
  fails by doing eight things adequately.

## Success metrics

In order. If two conflict, the higher one wins.

1. **Attributed MRR sourced by you.** The number.
2. **Closed deals, by segment.** Tells us where to spend the next quarter.
3. **Pipeline coverage.** Open opportunity value against next quarter's target. Under 3x means we
   have a top of funnel problem, not a closing problem.
4. **Conversion rate by stage.** Where deals die.
5. **90 day retention on the accounts you closed.** A close that churns in month two was not a close.
6. **Speed to first touch.** Inbound lead to first human contact. Should be measured in hours.

Not metrics: emails sent, calls made, posts published, followers, impressions. I will not ask about
any of them and you should not report them.

## How this grows

- **Growth Advisor.** Trial shape. Your own accounts only. Not what is being proposed here, but it is
  what the system supports if we want a slower start.
- **Head of Growth.** What I am proposing. Whole pipeline, one to one email, your own accounts to
  edit, commission on everything you source.
- **Employed.** Post conversion. Full pipeline, the mailing list, a real base, revised equity, and a
  say in who gets hired next.
- The move between them is a conversation with a date on it, not something you have to ask for
  repeatedly. Attributed MRR is the trigger and it is visible to both of us on the same screen.

## Boundaries

- **Brand voice.** Say what the thing is. No hype, no superlatives, no invented urgency. If a claim
  needs a qualifier to be true, it is the wrong claim.
- **Three things we do not say, ever.** We are not a general AI employee. We do not connect to
  everything. We do not run productions autonomously. Each one invites a comparison we lose or a
  promise we cannot keep.
- **No discounts without my approval.** Published pricing is published pricing. Promo codes exist and
  they are yours to issue inside the limits we agree. Anything outside that comes to me first.
- **No promises about the roadmap.** Not a date, not a feature, not "it's coming". If a deal depends
  on something that does not exist, bring it to me and I will decide whether to commit. Sometimes I
  will. It has to be my decision because I am the one who has to build it.
- **No contract terms, indemnities or legal language** agreed on a call. Everything legal comes back
  to me.
- **Customer data stays in the CRM.** Not in your personal spreadsheets, not exported to a third
  party tool.
- **If you are unsure whether something crosses a line, it does.** Ask.
