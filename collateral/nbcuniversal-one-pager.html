Prepared for NBCUniversal

Know who is on which shoot, and who is running it.

ABRAM is one production workspace holding crew assignments, upcoming shoot days, the person overseeing each one, the details of the day, and the schedule underneath all of it. The answer is on screen.

The five things you raised
01

Team production assignments

  • Every person on the roster shows booked hours against their weekly capacity, a utilization gauge, and the projects they are currently assigned to.
  • Double bookings, overcommitments and capacity overages are counted as conflicts on one screen, while there is still time to move someone.
  • One timeline carries every crew member and every kit, grouped by department. Bookings drag to new dates, and rescheduling a work order moves its kit as a group.
02

Upcoming shoots and projects

  • A shoot day is a work order: dates, times, location, crew, gear, and a running cost checked against the package budget as you build it.
  • Status keeps itself current — Draft, Confirmed, In Progress, Wrapped — as the dates arrive. Confirming reserves the gear and places holds on crew calendars; wrapping releases both.
  • Each project opens on a timeline preview, budget snapshot, team capacity and upcoming events, with a full Gantt and workload view a tab away.
03

Who is overseeing the shoot

  • The project header carries the client, the budget, the due date, how many people are booked, and the current status on one line.
  • Roles and per-person permissions decide who sees rates and budgets, and who is scoped to their assigned projects only. Custom roles can be defined beyond the standard set.
  • Every segment of a run of show has an owner, and every change to a project lands in an activity feed with the comment thread attached.
04

Details of the shoot

  • Call sheets hold general info, locations, the schedule with eighths, personnel call times and department notes in one document. Build one from a work order and that day's crew, resources and location come across with it.
  • Autofill from the project, or have ABRAM draft the sheet for you to review. Preview, print, and send to selected crew and outside email addresses.
  • For live and broadcast, the Run of Show is a minute-by-minute segment grid with column presets per role — Producer, Camera, Audio, Graphics, Replay, Stage Manager. Go Live tracks elapsed against scheduled, so the variance is visible while the show is running.
05

Scheduling

  • Bookings are Tentative, Confirmed or Declined. A tentative hold still protects the slot.
  • Adding someone already booked raises a flag with a per-day conflict timeline. Gear that is already out is a hard stop, so two units cannot claim the same package.
  • Google Calendar and Outlook sync inbound, so outside commitments arrive as busy blocks with nobody re-entering them. A booking on a production the viewer does not own shows as Busy, details redacted.
Also in the workspace

Script breakdown and stripboard scheduling with day-out-of-days · equipment, kit and vehicle inventory · timesheets that pre-fill from the work order · deliverable review with Frame.io inside the project · budgets, invoicing and a client portal · Slack notifications for approvals and changes.

Where a pilot starts

One show. Load its roster, enter the next month of shoot days as work orders, and run a single call sheet end to end. Everything above is in the product today.
